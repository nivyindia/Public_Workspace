# 💰 Positive Reply & Interested Lead Flow

This page covers exactly what to do the moment a lead responds positively. Most conversion leakage happens here — VAs get a reply, get excited, and either say too much, go silent, or forget to update the CRM. Follow this flow precisely every time.

> This is the highest-leverage moment in your entire job. One wrong move here = lost revenue. One right move = booked call.
> 

> 📱 **Getting a positive reply on WhatsApp, Email, or Instagram?** The exact same flow on this page applies to every platform. For platform-specific reply templates, go to [Outreach Script Library](https://www.notion.so/33de5082b9d4816db634f826d1c981fd) — Sections 3 (WhatsApp), 4 (Email), and 5 (Instagram).
>

# Part 1 — When a Lead Replies Positively in Chat

A "positive reply" means any of the following:

- "Yes, tell me more"
- "How does this work?"
- "What's the price?"
- "Sounds interesting"
- "We could use help with that"
- "Send me more info"
- Any question about your service
- Any reply that isn't a NO

> If they replied at all, treat it as warm. Even a one-word reply is an opening.
> 

---

## Step 1 — Reply within 5 minutes

Speed is your biggest advantage. Most competitors take hours or days. You reply in minutes.

**Rule:** If you see a reply, stop everything else and respond first.

Max acceptable delay:

| Time of day | Max reply time |
| --- | --- |
| During work hours | 5–15 minutes |
| End of shift | Next morning, first thing |
| Weekend (if applicable) | Monday morning |

> Never leave a positive reply unread overnight without flagging it to your supervisor.
> 

---

## Step 2 — Do NOT pitch immediately

The moment they show interest, your instinct will be to explain everything. Resist this.

**Wrong approach:**

> "Great! So we offer lead generation, accounting, digital marketing, website development, automation..."
> 

**Right approach:** Ask one question to understand their situation first.

### Reply templates by situation

**If they said "Tell me more" or "How does this work?"**

> "Happy to! Quick question first — what's your biggest challenge right now with [lead gen / accounting / growth]? That way I can make sure what I share is actually relevant to you."
> 

**If they asked "What's the price?"**

> "Good question — it depends on what you actually need. Can I ask, are you currently doing [the thing] in-house or working with anyone for it?"
> 

> Never send pricing in a DM. Always move to a call first.
> 

**If they said "Sounds interesting" or gave a vague positive**

> "Glad it resonated! What part caught your attention — is [lead gen / accounting / automation] something you're actively looking at right now?"
> 

**If they asked a specific question about the service**

Answer briefly (1–2 lines max), then redirect:

> "[Short answer]. Are you looking to sort this out soon or still exploring options?"
> 

---

## Step 3 — Qualify in the conversation (2 questions max)

Once they respond to your first question, you need to confirm two things before booking a call:

1. **Do they actually need what you offer?** — Is there a real problem you can solve?
2. **Are they the decision maker?** — Can they actually say yes to a deal?

**Qualification questions (pick the most natural one):**

- "Are you the one handling this, or is there someone else involved in the decision?"
- "Is this something you're looking to sort out in the next few weeks?"
- "Are you currently working with anyone for this, or starting fresh?"

If they pass both — they need the service AND they're the decision maker — move immediately to booking.

If they're NOT the decision maker:

> "Got it — would it make sense to loop them in on a quick call so we're all on the same page?"
> 

---

## Step 4 — Book the call

Do not ask "Do you want to get on a call?" — it sounds like work. Frame it as a quick, low-pressure conversation.

**Booking script:**

> "Honestly, the easiest thing would be a quick 15-minute call — I can show you exactly what would work for your situation and you can decide from there. No pressure at all. Does [day] or [day] work for you?"
> 

If they say yes to a time:

> "Perfect. Let me send you the calendar link — you can lock in a time that works."
> 

> [Send Calendly link]
> 

If they're unsure about timing:

> "Totally fine — here's the link, just grab any slot that works for you when you're ready."
> 

> [Send Calendly link]
> 

**After they book:**

> "Confirmed! Looking forward to it. I'll send a reminder the day before. If anything comes up, just let me know."
> 

---

## Step 5 — Update CRM immediately (same minute)

The moment a call is booked, open the CRM and update:

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| Status | Interested → Booked |
| Last contact date | Today's date |
| Next follow-up date | Day before the call (for reminder) |
| Notes | Their main problem, what they're interested in, any details from the conversation |
| Call date & time | The booked slot |

> If you close the chat without updating the CRM, assume the lead is lost. The closer needs this information before the call.
>

# Part 2 — When CRM Status = "Interested" (but no call booked yet)

This status means: they engaged positively but a call has not been confirmed yet. This is the most common place leads go cold and die.

**Why this happens:**

- VA got a positive reply, said they'd follow up, and forgot
- Lead said "let me check my schedule" and the VA never followed up
- Lead asked for "more info" and the VA sent a wall of text — lead went cold
- Call was booked but then lead cancelled and VA never rebooked

---

## The Interested Lead Follow-Up Sequence

If status = Interested and no call is booked, follow this sequence without exception:

### Day 0 — Initial positive reply received

Send the booking message (see Part 1 Step 4). Update CRM to Interested.

### Day 1 — No call booked yet

If they didn't book from your Calendly link:

> "Hey [Name], just checking — did you get a chance to grab a time? Happy to suggest a slot if that's easier."
> 

### Day 3 — Still no booking

> "Hey [Name], still happy to connect whenever works for you. This week or next work for me — just let me know what suits you best."
> 

### Day 6 — Final attempt before moving to cold nurture

> "Hey [Name], I'll leave this here for now — if you ever want to revisit, just drop me a message and I'll be right back. Good luck with everything in the meantime!"
> 

After Day 6 with no response → update CRM status to **No Response** and add a note: "Was interested, did not book. Re-approach in 3–4 weeks."

---

## What NOT to do when status = Interested

| Wrong | Why it kills the deal |
| --- | --- |
| Sending long explanations of the service | Overwhelms them, gives them a reason to say "let me think" |
| Asking "Are you still interested?" | Puts them on the spot, usually gets a no |
| Disappearing after one follow-up | Leads go cold within 48 hours |
| Sending pricing without a call | They'll say "too expensive" before understanding the value |
| Sending voice notes or long audio | Too much commitment for a cold lead |
| Waiting more than 24 hours to follow up | You will lose them |

# Part 3 — After the Call is Booked (Status = "Booked")

Booking the call is not the finish line. Your job continues until the call actually happens.

---

## What to do immediately after booking

### 1. Prepare and send the call handover to your supervisor / closer

Fill in the handover format and submit to your supervisor the same day the call is booked.

**Handover format:**

Client Name: | Company name: | Industry / Niche: | Country: | Decision maker? (Yes / No / Unknown): | Main problem / what they mentioned: | Services they're interested in: | Conversation summary (2–3 sentences): | Call date & time: | Time zone: | Call link sent? (Yes / No): | CRM updated? (Yes / No):

**Example:**

Client Name: James Mitchell | Company: Peak Roofing LLC | Industry: Roofing | Country: USA (Texas) | Decision maker: Yes (Owner) | Main problem: Getting more local leads, currently only referrals | Services interested in: Lead generation + Google Ads | Conversation summary: Replied to LinkedIn DM asking about lead gen. Engaged well, said referrals are slowing down. Wants consistent pipeline. Agreed to 15-min call. | Call date & time: Thursday 10 April, 3:00 PM EST | Time zone: EST | Call link sent: Yes (Google Meet) | CRM updated: Yes

Submit this to: **[your supervisor — WhatsApp group / Notion / wherever specified]**

---

### 2. Send the calendar confirmation to the lead

If you used Calendly, this is automatic. If you booked manually:

> "Hi [Name], confirming our call for [Day, Date] at [Time] [Timezone]. Here's the meeting link: [Google Meet / Zoom link]. Looking forward to speaking with you!"
> 

---

### 3. Send a reminder the day before

> "Hi [Name], just a reminder about our call tomorrow at [Time] [Timezone]. Here's the link: [meeting link]. See you then!"
> 

---

### 4. Send a same-day reminder (1 hour before)

> "Hi [Name], our call is in about an hour — [Time] [Timezone]. Here's the link again: [meeting link]. Looking forward to it!"
> 

---

### 5. If they don't show up

Wait 10 minutes past the call start time, then message:

> "Hi [Name], looks like we missed each other — no worries at all. Shall we rebook for another time? Happy to find a slot that works better for you."
> 

If they don't respond within 48 hours:

> "Hey [Name], just following up in case life got busy. Totally understand — whenever you're ready, just let me know and we'll find a time."
> 

Update CRM: Status → **No Show**. Add note with rebook attempt dates. Flag to supervisor.

---

## CRM Status Summary — The Full Flow

New → Messaged (you sent first message) → Replied (they responded) → Interested (positive reply, call not yet booked) → Booked (call confirmed, handover sent) → [Closer takes over]

No Response — ghosted at any stage, re-approach in 3–4 weeks

No Show — booked but didn't attend, attempt rebook ×2

> Your responsibility ends when the closer picks up the call. Everything before that — from first message to confirmed handover — is your job.
>
