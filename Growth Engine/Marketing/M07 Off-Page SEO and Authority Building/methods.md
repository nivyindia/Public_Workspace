# M07 — Methods

## M07.1 — Link-Worthy Asset Identification

- **Manual:** Review the content already produced by M09 and ask: would an independent site genuinely want to reference this? Original data/research, comprehensive guides, and free tools/templates tend to earn links; generic sales-oriented pages almost never do.
- **AI-assisted:** An LLM can help brainstorm "linkable asset" formats (e.g., an original survey, a free calculator, an industry benchmark report) based on the M03 pillars — treat this as idea generation, not a finished plan.

## M07.2 — Outreach & Link Building

- **Manual:** Identify sites that have linked to comparable content (competitor backlink gap analysis, free-tool version) or that publish in the same topic space, and send a personalized outreach email explaining why the asset is genuinely useful to their audience — not a generic mass template.
- **Free-tool:** Ahrefs' and Moz's free/limited backlink checkers (both offer a capped free lookup — verify current limits) show a sample of who links to a competitor, as a starting prospect list.
- **Standing rule:** No purchased links, no link farms, no reciprocal-linking schemes at scale — these violate Google's link spam guidelines and risk a manual action; every link in the tracker should be earned through genuine outreach or organic mention.

## M07.3 — Digital PR & Guest Content

- **Manual:** Monitor journalist-request platforms (HARO-style services) for queries relevant to the business's expertise; respond with a genuinely useful, specific answer, not a generic pitch. Pitch guest posts to relevant industry blogs with a specific, non-generic angle.
- **Free-tool:** Free tiers of journalist-request platforms — verify current terms, since some have shifted from fully free to paid/freemium over time.

## M07.4 — Directory & Citation Building

- **Manual:** List the business on Google Business Profile (free, and especially important if there's any local/regional relevance) and a small number of genuinely relevant, reputable industry directories — not a mass-submission to hundreds of low-quality directories, which provides little value and some spam risk.
- **Free-tool:** Google Business Profile (free). Industry-specific directories vary by vertical — identify the 5–10 that are actually respected in the business's specific niche rather than submitting broadly.

## M07.5 — Backlink Monitoring & Disavow Hygiene

- **Manual:** Periodically review the backlink profile (via Search Console's "Links" report, which is free and shows the domain's actual known backlinks) for anything obviously spammy (link farms, unrelated foreign-language sites in bulk, clearly purchased-link patterns).
- **Free-tool:** Google Search Console's Links report is the free, authoritative source for a domain's own backlink data.
- **Standing rule:** Disavowing is a rarely-needed, higher-risk tool — used it only for a clear, documented pattern of spammy links, not preemptively or on a hunch, since misuse can remove the value of legitimate links.
