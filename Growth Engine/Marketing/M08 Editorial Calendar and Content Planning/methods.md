# M08 — Methods

## M08.1 — Calendar Structure & Cadence Planning

- **Manual:** Start from actual team capacity (how many hours/week are genuinely available for content production across writing, editing, and design), not a target number pulled from a competitor's cadence. A realistic 1 post/week beats an abandoned 3 posts/week.
- **Free-tool:** A shared Notion/Airtable/Google Sheets calendar view is sufficient — no dedicated calendar software is required to run this stage.

## M08.2 — Funnel-Stage Content Mix

- **Manual:** Review the M04 keyword tracker's funnel-stage tags and deliberately schedule a mix — a calendar that's 90% awareness-stage content generates traffic but few conversions; one that's 90% decision-stage content has nothing to attract new visitors. A rough starting ratio (e.g., roughly balanced across the three stages, adjusted to the business's actual funnel data once M21 reporting exists) is a reasonable default, not a fixed rule.
- **AI-assisted:** An LLM can suggest a starting ratio based on general funnel principles, but the business's own conversion data (once available from M21) should override a generic suggestion.

## M08.3 — Editorial Workflow & Role Assignment

- **Manual:** Define each step explicitly — Brief (from M04's keyword-to-content handoff template) → Draft (M09) → Edit/Review → SEO pass (M05) → Publish → Distribute (M10). Assign a named owner per step, even if one person wears multiple hats on a small team; an unassigned step is where things stall.
- **Checklist gate:** No piece moves to "Publish" status without the review step completed — see `checklists.md`.

## M08.4 — Cross-Channel Sequencing

- **Manual:** When a piece is scheduled to publish, also schedule its social repurposing (M10 → M11–M16) and newsletter feature (M17) for the same week, not as an afterthought weeks later when the content is no longer fresh.
- **Automated:** Once the calendar lives in Airtable/Notion, a linked-record view can show "publish this week" alongside "repurpose/distribute this week" so the connection isn't manual memory-dependent.

## M08.5 — Calendar Maintenance & Backlog Grooming

- **Manual:** Weekly review: what published on schedule, what slipped and why, what's coming up in the next 2 weeks, what new ideas (from M04/M03) need triaging into the backlog vs. scheduling directly.
- **Standing rule:** A calendar that's persistently missed is a capacity-planning problem (fix in M08.1), not a discipline problem to push through — repeatedly missing dates is a signal to reduce cadence, not a reason to add pressure.
