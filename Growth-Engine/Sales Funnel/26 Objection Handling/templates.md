## The ARP Framework
**The Golden Rule: never argue. Always Acknowledge → Reframe → Probe.**
- **Acknowledge:** "That's completely fair / I understand that concern"
- **Reframe:** "Here's a different way to look at it..."
- **Probe:** "What would need to be true for you to move forward?"

## Example Library Entries (representative — full library grows over time)

**Price — "It's too expensive"**
> "That's fair — let me ask: compared to what? If [service] saves you 20 hours a week, and your time is worth even $50/hour, that's real monthly value for a fraction of the cost. Does that math work for you?"

**Trust — "I've never heard of [Company]"**
> "Fair — we're a growing team. I'll share 2-3 case studies from clients in your industry, and you can speak with one of them directly. Would that help?"

**Timing — "I need to think about it"**
> "Of course! What specifically would be helpful to think through? I'd rather answer any remaining questions now than have you sitting with uncertainty. Is it the price, the process, or something else?"

**Competition — "We're already using [competitor]"**
> "That's great — what do you love about them? [listen] And is there anything you wish was different? [listen] Based on what you just described, we might actually be a good fit for [the gap they mentioned]."

**Quality/Risk — "What if it doesn't work out?"**
> "We offer a trial period and a replacement guarantee if it's not a fit. You have very little to lose and a lot of time to gain."

**Commitment — "I don't want a long-term contract"**
> "We operate month-to-month after the first period — no lock-in, no penalty for leaving. We earn your continued business every month."
