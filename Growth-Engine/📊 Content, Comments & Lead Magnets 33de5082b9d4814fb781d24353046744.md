# 📊 Content, Comments & Lead Magnets

This page covers the three activities that turn your profile from a static page into an inbound lead generation machine: content posting, comment activity, and lead magnets. These are not optional extras — they are the difference between a dead profile and one that generates warm leads alongside your outreach.

---

## Why content matters for outreach

When a prospect receives your connection request or DM, the first thing they do is visit your profile. If your last post was 3 months ago, or you have no posts at all, you look inactive and untrustworthy. Active profiles with consistent content get 2–3x higher connection acceptance rates and significantly more positive replies.

Content also does something outreach alone cannot: it generates inbound. Prospects who find your post through a hashtag or shared post can message you first — the easiest leads you will ever convert.

## 2. Weekly Posting Schedule

Post a minimum of 3 times per week. Use this content mix to avoid looking promotional:

| Day | Post Type | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Industry insight | Educate your audience, build authority |
| Wednesday | Business tip or how-to | Provide value, increase saves/shares |
| Friday | Conversation post or opinion | Drive comments, boost algorithmic reach |

**Content ratio to follow:**

- 40% Education (how-to, guides, explainers)
- 30% Insights & trends (industry news, observations)
- 20% Case studies & proof (results, client outcomes — kept anonymous)
- 10% Direct service promotion

> Never post promotional content more than once per week. Prospects tune out profiles that only talk about their services.

## 5. Comment Activity Strategy

Commenting is one of the highest-leverage activities on LinkedIn. When you comment on a post by a founder, CEO, or agency owner, your name and headline appear in their notifications and in front of their entire audience.

**Daily target: 5–10 comments on posts by your ideal prospects**

**Who to comment on:**

- Founders and business owners
- CEOs of SMEs and agencies
- Roofing, HVAC, and home service business owners
- Consultants and coaches
- Finance professionals and CPAs

**Four comment types that build authority:**

**Insight comment** — extend the post's idea with your own knowledge

*"Great point. Many businesses underestimate how automation can streamline operations — we've seen companies save 10–20 hours weekly just by implementing basic CRM and workflow automation."*

**Value-add comment** — introduce a related problem or observation

*"This is very true. One thing we often see is businesses focusing entirely on lead generation while their internal systems — CRM, financial reporting, operations — aren't ready to handle the growth."*

**Conversation comment** — ask a thoughtful question to start a dialogue

*"Interesting perspective. Do you think AI will replace traditional bookkeeping entirely, or will it simply enhance how accountants work?"*

**Authority comment** — share a pattern you observe across clients

*"Many growing companies reach the point where outsourcing accounting and automation isn't optional — it's the only way to scale without burning out the founding team."*

> Never comment generic phrases like "Great post!" or "Thanks for sharing." These add zero value and signal low quality to the algorithm.
