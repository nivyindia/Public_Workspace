## The Golden Rule of Objection Handling

An objection is not a rejection. It is a question in disguise. Most objections mean one of three things:

- "I don't understand the value yet"
- "I don't trust you yet"
- "Now is not the right time"

Your job is to find out which one and address it. Never argue. Never pressure. Always acknowledge, then redirect.

**Framework for every objection:**

1. Acknowledge — show you heard them
2. Clarify — understand the real concern
3. Redirect — reframe toward the call

## Category 1 — "Not Interested" Objections

### Objection 1: "Not interested."

**What it really means:** They haven't seen a reason to be interested yet. This is the most common deflection — not a real decision.

**Response:**

> *"Completely understand — I'd feel the same way without knowing the details. I'm not looking to pitch anything, just to understand if there's a fit. What's your current setup for [accounting / marketing / automation]? If it's working well, totally fine — just want to check.*"
> 

**If they still say no:** Send a one-line follow-up 3 days later: *"No problem at all. If things change, feel free to reach out."* Then re-engage in 30 days.

---

### Objection 2: "We're not looking for this right now."

**Response:**

> *"That makes sense — timing matters. When would be a better time to reconnect? We work with a lot of businesses who weren't actively looking but found it worth a 15-minute conversation to see what was possible. Would [month] be a better window?"*
> 

---

### Objection 3: "I get too many messages like this."

**Response:**

> *"You're right — there's a lot of noise out there and I appreciate you being direct. I'll keep it brief: [one sentence on specific value]. If that's relevant to you, happy to share more. If not, no problem at all."*
> 

---

### Objection 4: "Stop messaging me."

**Response:**

> *"Absolutely — no more messages from me. Sorry for any inconvenience."*
> 

Then mark as **Do Not Contact** in CRM immediately. Never follow up again.

## Category 2 — Timing Objections

### Objection 5: "We're too busy right now."

**Response:**

> *"Totally get it — busy is usually a good sign. The businesses we help most are actually the ones that are growing fast but running into friction with [bookkeeping / leads / operations]. When does it make sense to reconnect — in 2 weeks or next month?"*
> 

---

### Objection 6: "Maybe in a few months."

**Response:**

> *"That works — I'll make a note to follow up then. Can I ask — what needs to happen between now and then before it makes sense to explore this?"*
> 

This question reveals the real blocker and gives you a hook for the follow-up.

---

### Objection 7: "We just started / We're too early for this."

**Response:**

> *"Actually, that's often the best time — getting the foundations right early saves a lot of fixing later. We work with a few early-stage businesses where [specific service] is already paying off. Worth a quick 15-minute call to see if it makes sense at your stage?"*
> 

---

## Category 3 — Price Objections

### Objection 8: "It's too expensive."

**Response:**

> *"I understand — that's a fair concern. Can I ask what you're currently spending on [this function]? A lot of our clients came to us after realising their current setup — whether in-house or other tools — was actually costing more when they added up the full picture. Happy to walk through a quick comparison if that's useful."*
> 

---

### Objection 9: "We don't have budget for this."

**Response:**

> *"That makes sense — budget is always a factor. Our packages are flexible and start quite lean. What would a realistic budget look like for something like this if you did move forward? That helps me understand if we have an option that fits."*
> 

---

### Objection 10: "Can you do it cheaper?"

**Response:**

> *"Let me check what's possible — sometimes we can adjust the scope to fit a tighter budget. What's the number you're working with? I'll see if we can structure something around that."*
> 

Then pass to supervisor — do not negotiate pricing yourself.

---

### Objection 11: "We found someone cheaper."

**Response:**

> *"That's fair — price matters. Can I ask who you're looking at? The difference with Nivy is [quality control, CPA oversight, dedicated team, turnaround time] — a lot of our clients came to us after a cheaper option didn't deliver. Happy to show you the difference in a quick call."*
>

## Category 4 — Trust & Credibility Objections

### Objection 12: "I've never heard of Nivy."

**Response:**

> *"Completely understandable — we're growing fast and focused more on results than on marketing ourselves. We work with [type of businesses] across the US, UK, and Australia. Happy to share some examples of what we've done if that helps build context."*
> 

Use a case study from the Case Studies page if you have it.

---

### Objection 13: "How do I know you'll deliver?"

**Response:**

> *"That's the right question to ask. The best thing I can do is connect you with our team directly so you can ask them exactly that. We also offer a short initial engagement so you can see the quality before committing long-term. Would a quick call to meet the team work?"*
> 

---

### Objection 14: "You're based in India, aren't you?"

**Response:**

> *"We have operations in India, yes — which is actually what allows us to offer enterprise-quality work at a fraction of the local cost. Our team is experienced in US/UK accounting standards and works in your timezone for key meetings. Most of our clients see this as an advantage, not a concern."*
> 

---

### Objection 15: "We had a bad experience with outsourcing before."

**Response:**

> *"I hear that a lot and I'm sorry that happened — bad experiences are unfortunately common because a lot of outsourcing is unstructured. The reason clients stick with Nivy is that we have a dedicated account manager, QC processes, and clear SLAs — not just a freelancer you hire and hope for the best. Would it be worth 15 minutes to see how we work differently?"*
>

## Category 5 — "We Already Have Someone" Objections

### Objection 16: "We already have an accountant."

**Response:**

> *"That's great — are they handling everything from bookkeeping to tax compliance and financial reporting, or mainly day-to-day entries? Most businesses we work with have an accountant but find there are gaps in reporting or compliance that we fill. Worth a quick check?"*
> 

---

### Objection 17: "We already have a marketing agency."

**Response:**

> *"Good to know — are you happy with the results you're getting? We often work alongside existing agencies on specific gaps like lead generation, automation, or paid ads. Not looking to replace anyone — just checking if there are areas where we could add value."*
> 

---

### Objection 18: "We handle this in-house."

**Response:**

> *"Makes sense — a lot of our clients started that way. The main reason they came to us was capacity — their in-house team was stretched and couldn't scale fast enough. Is that something you're facing, or is it working well at your current size?"*
> 

---

### Objection 19: "We use [software tool] / We're on a platform already."

**Response:**

> *"We actually integrate with most major tools — QuickBooks, Xero, HubSpot, Salesforce, and others. So it wouldn't mean switching anything. We'd work within what you already have. Would it be useful to see how that works?"*
>

## Category 6 — Information & Delay Objections

### Objection 20: "Send me more information."

**What it really means:** They're not convinced yet but don't want to say no. Sending a brochure rarely converts.

**Response:**

> *"Happy to — to make sure I send you the most relevant info, can I ask: is your main focus [accounting / marketing / automation]? And are you looking for this for [industry/size of business]? That way I can send you exactly what's useful rather than a generic deck."*
> 

Then send a focused message, not everything. Follow up 2 days later.

---

### Objection 21: "I'll think about it."

**Response:**

> *"Of course — take your time. Can I ask what you'd need to see or know to feel comfortable moving forward? That helps me make sure you have everything you need to make a decision."*
> 

---

### Objection 22: "Can you send a proposal first?"

**Response:**

> *"Absolutely — our proposals are tailored to each business so they're most useful after a short discovery call where we understand your situation. It takes 15–20 minutes. That way the proposal actually fits your needs rather than being a generic document. Would [day] or [day] work?"*
> 

---

### Objection 23: "I need to speak to my partner / manager first."

**Response:**

> *"Of course — that makes sense. Would it be useful to have a call with both of you together? That way you both have the same information and can make a decision together. I can set that up at a time that works for you both."*
> 

---

## Category 7 — Niche / Relevance Objections

### Objection 24: "We're not in the industries you work with."

**Response:**

> *"We actually work across a wide range of industries — [accounting / marketing / automation] is relevant to most businesses regardless of sector. Can I ask what you do? I can tell you quickly whether we've worked in your space or have a relevant example."*
> 

---

### Objection 25: "We're too small for this."

**Response:**

> *"That's actually one of the main reasons businesses come to us — smaller companies get the same quality of work without the cost of enterprise providers. Our entry-level packages are built for growing businesses. Would it be worth seeing what's possible at your size?"*
>

## Quick Reference — Objection by Type

| Objection Type | Core Response Strategy |
| --- | --- |
| Not interested | Ask about their current setup — create curiosity |
| Bad timing | Agree, set a follow-up date, ask what needs to happen first |
| Too expensive | Reframe cost vs current spend — ask what budget looks like |
| No trust / unknown brand | Offer a case study or short trial engagement |
| Already have someone | Find the gap — complement, don't replace |
| Send info / think about it | Ask what they need to decide — keep conversation open |
| Too small / wrong industry | Reframe as perfect fit, share a relevant example |

## Things to Never Say

- Never say: *"Just 5 minutes of your time."* (Sounds desperate, will be ignored)
- Never say: *"I promise you won't regret it."* (Unverifiable, builds no trust)
- Never argue with a prospect or tell them they're wrong
- Never drop the price yourself — always pass to supervisor
- Never send a long wall of text in response to a short objection — keep responses short and conversational
