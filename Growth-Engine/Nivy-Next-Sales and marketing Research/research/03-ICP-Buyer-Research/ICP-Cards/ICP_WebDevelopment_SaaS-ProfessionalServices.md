# ICP Card — Web Development (Primary Segment: SaaS & Professional Services Crossover)

**Module:** 03 — ICP & Buyer Research | **Status:** Draft v1 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-06

## Firmographics
| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Company size | 5–150 employees |
| Revenue band | $500K–$25M |
| Industry | SaaS (marketing site, app UI), professional services (credibility-driven site), e-commerce (storefront) |
| Growth stage | Any — this is a cross-cutting need, not stage-specific |
| Geography | All 5 target countries; USA and UK highest volume |
| Tech stack signal | Outdated WordPress/Squarespace site, or no site refresh in 2+ years, or a broken/slow site flagged in analytics |
| Current solution | DIY builder (Wix/Squarespace), a freelancer who's gone unresponsive, or an aging site nobody owns internally |

## Buying Committee
| Role | Concern |
|---|---|
| Founder/Owner (economic buyer, smaller firms) | Site reflects credibility, converts visitors, doesn't want a multi-month project |
| Marketing lead (champion, larger firms) | Site performance/SEO, ease of ongoing content updates, design quality |
| IT/technical stakeholder (influencer, larger firms) | Security, hosting, integration with existing tools (CRM, analytics) |

## Top 3 Decision Criteria
1. **Portfolio quality and relevance** — buyers want to see sites built for similar company types (SaaS vs. professional services vs. e-commerce look very different), not a generic portfolio.
2. **Timeline certainty** — a defined, realistic delivery date is a bigger trust signal than the lowest price; horror stories about endless delays are common in this category.
3. **Post-launch ownership clarity** — who updates the site after launch? Buyers are wary of being locked out of their own site or dependent on the vendor for every small change.

## Top 3 Objections (anticipated — validate via VoC interviews)
1. "We've been burned before — a freelancer/agency disappeared mid-project." → Counter with milestone-based payment structure and a named project owner throughout.
2. "Will we be able to update the site ourselves after launch?" → Counter with a CMS-based build (not a hand-coded black box) plus a short handover training session.
3. "How do we know the site will actually perform (speed, SEO) and not just look good?" → Counter with technical performance benchmarks (load time, Core Web Vitals) built into the delivery scope, not treated as an afterthought.

## Preferred Channel
SEO/content (ironically, buyers searching "web development company" are found via the exact channel being sold), referral, and marketplaces (Clutch) for portfolio-driven discovery.

## Recommended Entry Offer
A fixed-price, fixed-timeline website package (e.g., "5-page site in 4 weeks") as the standard entry point, with a clear post-launch care-plan retainer as the natural upsell (feeding into ongoing SEO/content relationship with Digital Marketing).
