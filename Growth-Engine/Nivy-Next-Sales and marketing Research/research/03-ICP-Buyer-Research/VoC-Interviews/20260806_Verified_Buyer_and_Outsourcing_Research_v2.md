## 1. What's actually verified (safe to cite or build messaging on)

**Gartner — B2B buyers spend 17% of their purchase journey meeting with suppliers; 5–6% with any single vendor when comparing several.** This is Gartner's own published research (2017 B2B Buyer Survey, n=750, still the basis for their current buyer-journey materials). It's real and widely cited.
- *Implication for Nivy Next:* your site, case studies, and reporting samples do more selling than any call. A prospect evaluating 3–4 agencies gives each rep a few minutes of real attention — the proposal and the website have to close most of the gap on their own.

**Deloitte Global Outsourcing Survey — the driver mix has shifted.** Deloitte's tracked survey (500+ executives) shows cost as the *primary* driver dropped from roughly 70% of respondents (2020) to about a third now, with talent access and agility rising to close the gap. This is a real, specific, defensible number — stronger than the vague "talent access: very strong" claim in the earlier note.
- *Implication:* "we're cheaper" is a weakening pitch on its own. Positioning around talent access and speed is aligned with where the market has actually moved, not just aspirational.

**Clutch as a source type is legitimate** — it does verified client interviews, not just star ratings — but I have not pulled specific review numbers for this file, and neither should outreach copy until someone has actually read and logged them (this is exactly what your `VoC-Interviews` file is doing correctly — extend that process, don't shortcut it with invented figures).

## 3. The India-specific layer (the piece the earlier notes gestured at but didn't build properly)

This is where your positioning work actually needs to happen — the earlier notes gave you generic "outsourcing benefits" tables that could apply to any offshore country. Here's what's specific to an India-based agency selling into Tier-1 markets (US/UK/Canada/Australia), built from your existing ICP cards plus the verified research above:

**The real objection sequence for an Indian agency (not a generic offshore one):**
1. *"Is this actually going to work, or am I about to waste two months?"* — this is the dominant fear, ahead of price. Gartner's low-supplier-access finding means you rarely get to talk someone out of this live; it has to be pre-answered on the site (process docs, named account owner, sample report before signing).
2. *"Will I be talking to a different person every time?"* — account continuity, not nationality, is what your own `VoC-Interviews` findings (driver #4, "team quality and account continuity") already show matters most in retention. This is a stronger, sourced version of the earlier note's generic "communication gap" fear.
3. *Price comes last, not first* — consistent across both the Deloitte shift (cost declining as primary driver) and your existing secondary VoC file (driver #5, "price justified by value").

**Recommended positioning**, grounded in the above rather than invented stats: lead with process maturity and a named point of contact, not cost — the market data supports this shift is real, not just good instinct.
