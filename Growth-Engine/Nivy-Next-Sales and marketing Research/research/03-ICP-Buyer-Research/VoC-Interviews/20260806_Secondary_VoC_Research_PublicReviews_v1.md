# Secondary VoC Research — Public Review Evidence (Non-Nivy-Next Sources)

**Module:** 03 — ICP & Buyer Research | **Status:** v1 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-06

## What this is and isn't

This is **secondary research**, not primary VoC. It synthesizes real, publicly published reviews and buyer-facing content about *other* digital marketing agencies (Clutch, G2, Trustpilot, procurement guides) to extract decision drivers, objections, and buying-journey patterns. It is a stand-in for the primary interviews in `VoC_Interview_Plan_and_Question_Bank_v1.md`, which are still blocked on real conversations (Founding_Client_and_VoC_Bridge_Plan_v1.md, Part 1).

**Rules followed:**
- Every source below is real and linked. Nothing here is attributed to a Nivy Next client — none exist yet.
- Findings are paraphrased, not lifted verbatim (copyright + accuracy — aggregator sites summarize, they don't hand you raw transcripts).
- Where a source only gives aggregate/qualitative signal (e.g., "87% of reviewers praised X") rather than a single verifiable case, it's marked as **pattern-level**, not a discrete case row — don't over-claim precision this research doesn't support.
- This does **not** replace primary VoC. Treat it as the working hypothesis layer that primary interviews should confirm or correct, same as the ICP cards.

## Aggregated Decision Drivers (ranked by how often they recur across sources)

1. **Communication speed and consistency.** The single most repeated praise pattern across Clutch listings — reviewers on Power Digital, Disruptive Advertising, and others consistently single out same-day responsiveness and proactive updates as the reason they stayed, not just why they signed. This shows up more as a retention driver than a pure acquisition one — worth noting in the Objection Playbook as much as the Decision-Criteria Matrix.
2. **Proactive problem-flagging, not just reporting.** Clutch reviews of agencies like Power Digital call out teams that surface a bad month *and* a mitigation plan unprompted, rather than waiting to be asked — this is described as the most valuable part of the relationship once trust is established.
3. **Relevant-industry proof over generic case studies.** Recurring across Clutch and the Practical Ecommerce hiring guide: buyers want references from businesses like theirs and are explicitly wary of agencies that can't speak concretely about past results.
4. **Team quality and account continuity**, called out by name in several Clutch reviews (e.g., a named project manager credited for keeping a project on track) — buyers value a consistent point of contact over a faceless team.
5. **Price justified by value, not lowest bid.** Clutch's Evolve Agency Group reviews explicitly frame this as loyalty despite a "significant annual investment" because quality justified the cost — consistent with the general pattern that price becomes the deciding factor only after trust is established, not before.
6. **Own-house credibility signal.** The hiring guides flag that buyers informally audit an agency's *own* marketing/website/SEO as a proxy for competence before ever taking a call.

## Aggregated Objections / Churn Triggers (ranked)

1. **Unresponsive or unaccountable communication.** The strongest negative-sentiment pattern in the Trustpilot data — reviewers describe being unable to reach any staff member despite repeated attempts, and issues being reported multiple times without resolution. This is the mirror image of decision-driver #1: communication is as much a churn cause as a purchase driver.
2. **Overstated experience/capability claims that don't hold up.** A Trustpilot reviewer for one agency described being told the vendor had specific technical connections and experience that ultimately weren't demonstrated, with the engagement ending unresolved.
3. **Recommendations given without implementation support.** Practical Ecommerce's "7 signs" piece names this directly: agencies that repeat the same advice cycle after cycle without helping the client act on it, or that quietly reduce deliverables from an initial strong start down to a monthly call and a report nobody reads.
4. **Contract/refund friction when things go wrong.** The Trustpilot cases both end in unresolved refund or contract-termination disputes — this maps directly to the "minimum contract term" and "what happens if it doesn't work" questions already listed in the primary VoC question bank.
5. **Jargon as a cover for thin expertise.** Called out explicitly in the Practical Ecommerce piece as a red flag pattern buyers learn to recognize.

## Buying Journey Pattern (procurement-guide corroboration)

The Marketing Agency Network selection guide frames agency selection as a structured, higher-stakes process precisely because a bad choice is costly and most buyers have little prior experience running it — which supports the multi-touchpoint, trust-before-price sequence already documented in the Master Blueprint and ICP cards, rather than a single-call close.

## Limitations

- No source here is Nivy Next's target buyer directly (small local-service SMBs like the dental-clinic example) — the review base skews toward mid-market/SaaS-adjacent agencies. Local-SMB-specific secondary research (Google Maps reviews of local agencies, r/smallbusiness threads) is a good next pass but wasn't productively searchable this round — Reddit's own search surfaced marketing-agency SEO content about Reddit as a channel, not buyer discussion threads.
- Trustpilot reviews are unverified (unlike Clutch/G2) — treated as directional, not statistically representative.
- This is one research pass, not the "100 rows" analysis described in the original brief. Treat as a seed, not a finished decision-intelligence database.
