Nivy Next has no clients yet, so neither can be filled with fabricated content — a case study attributed to a client that doesn't exist is a testimonial fraud risk (FTC/ASA-type rules apply even for a small agency, and one prospect asking "can I talk to them?" ends the relationship). ## Part 1 — VoC Substitute That Doesn't Require Being a Client

The VoC plan (Deliverable 5) already includes a segment for exactly this situation: **"cold ICP-fit non-customers."** You don't need paying clients to run this — you need 20-30 minute conversations with people who match the ICP cards.

### Who to talk to (in order of ease)
1. **Your existing network** — anyone who runs or works at a SaaS/e-commerce/professional-services company matching an ICP card, even loosely. Ask for 20 minutes as "market research," not a sales call.
2. **Warm second-degree contacts** — ask your network for one introduction each to someone who fits an ICP.
3. **Cold outreach for research only** — a request for a 15-minute conversation converts far better than a sales pitch. Frame: "I'm building a company to solve [pain] and want to make sure I'm solving the real problem before I build the pitch."

### What to ask (same coding framework as the VoC plan, minus "why did you choose us")
- How do you currently handle [service line]? What's frustrating about it?
- What would make you seriously consider bringing in an outside team/vendor for this?
- What would make you *not* trust an outside/offshore team with this?
- What's the last vendor decision like this you made — what tipped it?
- If price weren't a factor, what would the ideal version of this look like?

This is customer-discovery interviewing, not win/loss interviewing — but it produces the same coded output (decision drivers, objections, verbatim language) the messaging house and ICP cards need to move from hypothesis to evidence. **Target: 8-10 of these across 2-3 ICPs before revising the ICP cards and messaging house.**

## Part 2 — Getting 3 Real Case Studies (Founding Client Program)

The fastest, most credible path used by every service business starting from zero: **run a small, explicit pilot program, and negotiate the case study into the deal itself.**

### The offer
- Recruit 3-5 "founding clients" — real prospects, ideally from your network or warm outreach — for a discounted or free pilot-scope engagement (use the "Entry Offer" tier already defined in Package_Pricing_AllServiceLines_v1.md — e.g., the Growth Audit, single-workflow automation pilot, or trial-period VA).
- In exchange for the discount, they agree upfront to: (a) a named case study with a quote, (b) a reference call for future prospects, (c) permission to use their logo.
- Get this in writing at signing, not after — asking for a testimonial after the fact has a much lower hit rate than agreeing to it as a term of the discount.

### Why this beats waiting
- CB Insights and most founder-led-sales research point the same direction: pilots with explicit success metrics and a documented outcome are what turn into the first defensible case studies — there's no shortcut that doesn't involve real delivery.
- A handful of even modest, honestly-reported pilot outcomes are worth more than polished-sounding fabricated ones — and they're the only kind that survive a prospect asking follow-up questions.

### Sourcing the first 3-5 pilots
- Direct outreach to your own network first (highest close rate, per the research: founders' first clients are almost always direct contacts).
- Consider one white-label/partner arrangement — subcontracting under an established agency's brand for the first engagement or two borrows their credibility while you build your own portfolio (common early-agency tactic).
- Use the cold email/LinkedIn sequences already built (05-Channels-Sales-Systems) but swap the CTA in Touch 1 for a "founding client" framing rather than a generic pitch — scarcity/cohort framing ("we're running a pilot cohort of 5 companies, X spots left") performs better than open-ended offers.

### What "done" looks like
- 3 completed pilot engagements, each with: a documented before/after metric (even directional), a client quote, and permission to publish.
- Feed each into the Content & SEO case-study process already defined in the Master Blueprint (Deliverable 6): Challenge → Approach → Result → Quote.
