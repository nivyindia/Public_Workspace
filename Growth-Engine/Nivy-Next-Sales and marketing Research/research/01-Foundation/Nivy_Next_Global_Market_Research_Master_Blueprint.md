## Deliverable 1 — Executive Overview

### Purpose of the Research
Nivy Next sells seven service lines across five countries to a wide buyer base. Without a structured research program, positioning, pricing, and sales messaging are guesswork. This program builds a single evidence base that drives every commercial decision: who to target, what to say, what to charge, and how to close.

### Strategic Objectives
1. Identify the highest-value ICP × country × service-line combinations to prioritize.
2. Build evidence-based positioning and messaging that outperforms local and offshore competitors.
3. Design pricing and packaging that match each country's buying norms.
4. Build a repeatable, documented sales and marketing engine (not founder-dependent).
5. Establish trust, compliance, and security assets required to win mid-market and enterprise deals.
6. Create a measurement system (KPIs/dashboards) so decisions are data-driven from month one.

### Key Business Questions
- Which 2–4 ICPs, per country, should Nivy Next pursue first, and why?
- What do buyers in each country actually weigh most: price, trust, speed, expertise, or compliance?
- What packaging and pricing model (retainer, project, hybrid) converts best per segment?
- Which channels (LinkedIn, SEO, referral, marketplace, outbound) produce the best CAC by country?
- What proof (case studies, certifications, security answers) is required before a buyer will sign?
- Where is Nivy Next currently losing deals, and to whom?

### Expected Business Outcomes
- A prioritized go-to-market sequence instead of "sell everything to everyone."
- Higher win rates from sharper messaging and objection-readiness.
- Reduced sales cycle length due to pre-built trust assets.
- Predictable CAC/LTV by channel and country, enabling confident budget allocation.
- A defensible position for future fundraising or partnership conversations.

### How This Research Improves the Business
| Area | Improvement Driven by This Research |
|---|---|
| Sales | Clear ICPs, buying-committee maps, and objection playbooks shorten cycles |
| Marketing | Country- and ICP-specific messaging and channel mix improve conversion |
| Pricing | Country-calibrated packages avoid underpricing tier-1 and overpricing tier-2 |
| Positioning | Differentiated narrative vs. local firms and offshore generalists |
| Operations | Documented SOPs and delivery workflows scale beyond the founder |

## Deliverable 2 — Complete Research Architecture

Each module below lists: **Objective, Key Questions, Data to Collect, Sources, Methods, Output.**

### 2.0 Market & Go-to-Market Research

**2.1 International Market Opportunity**
- *Objective:* Size the addressable market for each service line across the five countries.
- *Key questions:* What is the market size/growth rate per service line per country? What macro trends (AI adoption, talent shortages, outsourcing acceptance) favor Nivy Next?
- *Data:* Market size estimates, industry growth reports, outsourcing adoption rates.
- *Sources:* Statista, IBISWorld, government statistics bureaus, industry association reports.
- *Methods:* Desk research, secondary data synthesis.
- *Output:* One-page opportunity snapshot per service line.

**2.2 Country Analysis (USA, Canada, UK, Australia, UAE)**
- *Objective:* Understand demand drivers, buyer norms, and market entry conditions per country.
- *Key questions:* What is unique about doing B2B outsourced services business in each country?
- *Data:* Regulatory environment, business culture, digital maturity, competitive density.
- *Sources:* Government trade portals, local business news, in-country advisors.
- *Methods:* Desk research + expert interviews.
- *Output:* Country snapshot sheets (see Deliverable 3).

**2.3 Industry Prioritization**
- *Objective:* Identify which industries (SaaS, e-commerce, healthcare, professional services, agencies) yield the best fit per service line.
- *Key questions:* Which industries have budget, urgency, and low switching friction?
- *Data:* Industry growth, digital spend benchmarks, existing client success patterns.
- *Sources:* Industry reports, CRM history, LinkedIn Sales Navigator.
- *Methods:* Scoring matrix (market size × fit × competition × margin potential).
- *Output:* Ranked industry priority list per country.

**2.4 ICPs by Country and Service Line**
- *Objective:* Define 2–4 Ideal Customer Profiles per service line per priority country.
- *Key questions:* Company size, revenue band, tech stack, growth stage, current solution for the problem.
- *Data:* Firmographic and technographic data.
- *Sources:* LinkedIn, Crunchbase, BuiltWith, Clearbit.
- *Methods:* Firmographic research + interviews with current best-fit clients.
- *Output:* ICP cards (see Deliverable 3 template).

**2.5 Buyer Behavior & Decision-Making Units**
- *Objective:* Map who is involved in the purchase decision and their concerns.
- *Key questions:* Who initiates, who evaluates, who approves budget? What triggers a search for a vendor?
- *Data:* Role titles, buying triggers, escalation paths.
- *Sources:* Client/prospect interviews, sales call recordings.
- *Methods:* Qualitative interviews, sales call review.
- *Output:* Buying committee map per ICP.

**2.6 Purchase Drivers & Decision Criteria**
- *Objective:* Rank what buyers weigh most (price, trust, speed, expertise, compliance) by country.
- *Data:* Ranked criteria per ICP.
- *Sources:* VoC interviews, win/loss interviews, surveys.
- *Methods:* Structured surveys + interview coding.
- *Output:* Decision-criteria matrix per ICP × country.

**2.7 Pricing Expectations**
- *Objective:* Determine what price points and models (retainer/project/hourly) feel credible per country.
- *Data:* Competitor pricing, client budget ranges, willingness-to-pay signals.
- *Sources:* Competitor sites, proposals, closed-deal data.
- *Methods:* Competitive pricing audit + win/loss price analysis.
- *Output:* Pricing corridor per package per country.

**2.8 Trust Signals**
- *Objective:* Identify what makes a foreign vendor feel "safe" to each buyer type.
- *Data:* Certifications, security posture, references, local presence expectations.
- *Sources:* Interviews, RFP/vendor questionnaires received.
- *Methods:* Qualitative synthesis.
- *Output:* Trust requirements checklist per country (feeds Deliverable 8).

**2.9 Channel Preferences**
- *Objective:* Determine where each ICP discovers and evaluates vendors.
- *Data:* Discovery channel, evaluation channel, preferred contact method.
- *Sources:* Interviews, CRM source-of-lead data, LinkedIn/SEO analytics.
- *Methods:* CRM analysis + surveys.
- *Output:* Channel priority map per ICP.

**2.10 Sales Funnel Behavior**
- *Objective:* Understand typical stage-to-stage conversion and time delays.
- *Data:* Funnel stage durations, drop-off points.
- *Sources:* CRM pipeline data.
- *Methods:* CRM funnel analysis.
- *Output:* Funnel benchmark report.

**2.11 Competitive Benchmarking**
- *Objective:* Understand direct, indirect, and offshore competitor positioning.
- *See Deliverable 4.*

**2.12 Localization**
- *Objective:* Ensure messaging, terminology, and compliance references are locally accurate.
- *See Deliverable 3 (per-country localization checklist).*

## Deliverable 3 — Country-Specific Research Frameworks

Each country module below is a repeatable template — fill this out per country during Phase 2 of the roadmap.

### 3.1 USA
- **Market attractiveness:** Largest addressable market; high digital spend; strong SaaS/e-commerce base.
- **Demand drivers:** Talent cost pressure, speed-to-market expectations, AI adoption race.
- **Key industries:** SaaS, e-commerce (Shopify), professional services, healthcare (non-clinical ops), real estate.
- **Buying behavior:** Fast-moving but risk-aware; case studies and references matter heavily; procurement kicks in above ~$5–10k/month for mid-market.
- **Regulatory considerations:** State-level data laws (e.g., CCPA), CAN-SPAM for email, standard MSAs expected.
- **Pricing norms:** Comfortable with monthly retainers ($2k–$15k+ typical SMB/mid-market range); value-based pricing accepted for strategic work.
- **Communication style:** Direct, ROI-led, fast follow-up expected.
- **Recommended positioning:** Speed + quality + AI-enabled efficiency at a lower cost basis than local agencies.
- **Recommended acquisition channels:** LinkedIn outbound, SEO/content, referral, Clutch/G2 listings.
- **Trust requirements:** Case studies, testimonials, clear security answers, US-time-zone overlap for calls.
- **Localization checklist:** US spelling, USD pricing, US-based case studies, familiarity with US tax/business terms (LLC, EIN) if relevant to messaging.

### 3.2 Canada
- **Market attractiveness:** Smaller but stable; strong SMB base; cultural proximity to US buyers.
- **Demand drivers:** Talent shortage in tech/creative roles; bilingual (English/French) market needs.
- **Key industries:** Professional services, SaaS, healthcare, real estate.
- **Buying behavior:** More consensus-driven and cautious than US; values long-term relationships.
- **Regulatory considerations:** PIPEDA (privacy), CASL (Canada's strict anti-spam law — stricter than CAN-SPAM).
- **Pricing norms:** Similar to US but slightly more price-sensitive; CAD pricing preferred.
- **Communication style:** Polite, relationship-first, less aggressive sales tolerance.
- **Recommended positioning:** Reliable long-term partner, compliance-aware (especially CASL for email).
- **Recommended acquisition channels:** Referral, LinkedIn, local business associations/chambers of commerce.
- **Trust requirements:** CASL-compliant outreach, Canadian client references if possible.
- **Localization checklist:** CAD pricing option, CASL-compliant email opt-in language, Canadian English spelling.

### 3.3 UK
- **Market attractiveness:** Mature, competitive market; strong compliance culture.
- **Demand drivers:** Cost pressure post-economic uncertainty; strong outsourcing acceptance.
- **Key industries:** Professional services, agencies (white-label demand), SaaS, healthcare admin.
- **Buying behavior:** Formal, procurement-heavy for mid-market+; references and case studies essential.
- **Regulatory considerations:** UK GDPR, PECR (email/marketing consent law).
- **Pricing norms:** GBP pricing; retainer model standard; comfortable with fixed-scope SOWs.
- **Communication style:** Understated, evidence-led, skeptical of overclaiming.
- **Recommended positioning:** Precision, compliance, and quiet competence over aggressive sales language.
- **Recommended acquisition channels:** LinkedIn thought leadership, SEO, industry associations, agency-partnership/white-label channel.
- **Trust requirements:** UK GDPR compliance statement, ICO registration awareness, UK-based or UK-hours point of contact.
- **Localization checklist:** British English spelling, GBP pricing, UK GDPR/PECR language in proposals.

### 3.4 Australia
- **Market attractiveness:** Smaller, high-value market; strong outsourcing acceptance due to local talent cost.
- **Demand drivers:** High local labor costs make offshore delivery attractive; strong SME digitalization push.
- **Key industries:** SMEs across e-commerce, professional services, real estate, healthcare.
- **Buying behavior:** Direct and pragmatic; faster decision cycles than UK; time-zone is a real friction point.
- **Regulatory considerations:** Australian Privacy Principles (APP), Spam Act 2003.
- **Pricing norms:** AUD pricing; value-for-money framing resonates strongly.
- **Communication style:** Informal but efficient; wants fast, clear answers.
- **Recommended positioning:** Cost-effective, high-quality alternative to expensive local talent, with async-friendly delivery to offset time-zone gap.
- **Recommended acquisition channels:** LinkedIn, referral, SEO, marketplaces (Upwork/Clutch for lower entry-point deals).
- **Trust requirements:** Clear async communication protocol, Spam Act–compliant outreach, AUD invoicing.
- **Localization checklist:** AUD pricing, Australian English spelling, explicit time-zone overlap windows stated in proposals.

### 3.5 UAE
- **Market attractiveness:** Fast-growing, relationship-driven market with high digital ad spend.
- **Demand drivers:** Rapid business growth, VAT/corporate tax compliance needs, strong government digitalization push.
- **Key industries:** E-commerce/retail, real estate, hospitality, SME trading companies.
- **Buying behavior:** Relationship and trust-first; local presence or strong references weigh heavily; faster decisions once trust is established.
- **Regulatory considerations:** UAE VAT, new UAE Corporate Tax regime, data residency expectations for some sectors.
- **Pricing norms:** AED or USD pricing accepted; negotiation is culturally expected.
- **Communication style:** Relationship-first, in-person or video-call preference before commitment; WhatsApp is a legitimate business channel.
- **Recommended positioning:** Trusted growth partner with regional understanding, fast turnaround, and flexible engagement.
- **Recommended acquisition channels:** Referral, LinkedIn, local business networking events, WhatsApp-based follow-up.
- **Trust requirements:** Strong references from similar-region clients, willingness to do video calls/demos, clarity on data handling.
- **Localization checklist:** AED/USD dual pricing, Arabic-language landing page consideration **[ADDED]**, VAT-aware invoicing.

## Deliverable 5 — Voice-of-Customer (VoC) Research Program

### Interview Plan
- **Segments to interview:** current happy clients, at-risk/churned clients, lost prospects (win/loss), and cold ICP-fit non-customers.
- **Volume target:** 5–8 interviews per segment per quarter, minimum 3 per country where feasible.
- **Format:** 20–30 minute video calls, recorded with consent.

### Survey Plan
- Short (5–7 question) NPS + qualitative follow-up survey sent post-project and quarterly to active retainer clients.

### Sample Questions
**Current clients:** What made you choose us over alternatives? What almost stopped you from signing? What result matters most to you? What would make you refer us?
**Lost prospects:** What vendor did you choose instead, and why? What concern did we not resolve? What would have changed your decision?
**Churned clients:** What triggered the decision to leave? What was missing in delivery or communication?

### Data Coding Framework
Tag every interview transcript by: Decision driver (price/trust/speed/expertise/compliance), Objection type, Channel of discovery, Country, Service line, Sentiment (positive/neutral/negative).

### Insight Synthesis Process
Monthly: aggregate coded tags into a ranked list of top 5 decision drivers and top 5 objections per ICP. Feed directly into messaging (Deliverable 2.20) and sales objection playbook (Deliverable 2.30).

### Messaging Extraction Method
Pull verbatim client language (their words, not ours) from interviews to use in web copy, case studies, and ad creative — buyers trust language that mirrors their own.

## Deliverable 12 — Governance & Review System

### Weekly Review Agenda (30 min)
1. Tracker status review — tasks completed, blocked, overdue.
2. Key metric snapshot (leads, pipeline, meetings booked).
3. Blockers requiring decisions.
4. Next week's top 3 priorities per owner.

### Monthly Steering Committee Review (60 min)
1. Phase progress vs. roadmap.
2. KPI dashboard review (all departments).
3. Key insights from VoC/win-loss this month.
4. Budget/resourcing check.
5. Decisions needed from leadership.

### Quarterly Strategy Review (half-day)
1. Full blueprint progress audit.
2. Country/ICP prioritization re-validation.
3. Competitive landscape shift review.
4. Pricing/packaging performance review.
5. Roadmap adjustment for next quarter.

## Deliverable 13 — Final Research Production Plan

### Research Sequence
1. Complete Phases 1–3 of the roadmap (Foundation → Market Intelligence → ICP/Buyer Research).
2. Validate findings internally before moving to positioning/offer design.
3. Run pilot GTM execution to test findings against real market response.
4. Fold pilot learnings back into the research before final production.

### Interview Sequence
VoC interviews → win/loss interviews → churned-client interviews → non-customer ICP interviews (in that priority order, since current clients are fastest to access and highest-signal).

### Data Validation
Cross-check qualitative findings (interviews) against quantitative signals (CRM data, website analytics, competitor pricing) before finalizing any claim in the report — no single data point should drive a strategic decision alone.

### Analysis Methods
Thematic coding for interviews; funnel/cohort analysis for CRM and marketing data; comparative matrix analysis for competitor and country data.

### Executive Synthesis
Condense all modules into a 10–15 slide executive summary: opportunity size, prioritized ICPs, positioning, pricing, channel strategy, and 12-month plan.

### Presentation Format
Three tailored outputs from the same research base:
1. **Investor-ready version** — market opportunity, differentiation, unit economics, growth roadmap.
2. **Sales-playbook version** — ICPs, objection handling, messaging, pricing, proposal templates.
3. **Country-specific GTM playbooks** — one per country, combining Deliverable 3 with the finalized messaging, pricing, and channel plan.
