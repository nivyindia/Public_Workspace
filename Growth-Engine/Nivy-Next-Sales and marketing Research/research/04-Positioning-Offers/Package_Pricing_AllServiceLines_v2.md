## 1. Digital Marketing — ⚠ Founder decision needed (unchanged pending decision)
| Tier | Scope | Indicative Monthly Price (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | 1 channel (SEO or paid), monthly reporting | $1,500–$2,500 |
| Growth | 2–3 channels, dedicated strategist, bi-weekly calls | $3,000–$6,000 |
| Scale | Full-funnel, weekly reporting, senior strategist | $6,000–$12,000+ |
| **Entry offer** | Fixed-price Growth Audit | $1,000–$2,000 one-time |

**The decision, made explicit:** EZ Rankings publishes real SEO pricing from $225/mo. Nivy Next's Starter floor is $1,500 — not a small gap, a different market segment entirely. Two lanes, pick one before quoting:
- **Lane A — Premium/US-comparable (current pricing, no change):** Position against US-market agencies ($1,500–$5,000/mo typical), not against India-based low-cost providers. Requires the pitch to justify the premium explicitly (senior strategist access, US-facing reporting cadence, etc.) rather than letting prospects compare line-by-line against EZ Rankings.
- **Lane B — Compete on the low end:** Add a sub-$500/mo "Lite" tier below Starter to capture price-sensitive leads before they go to EZ Rankings, while keeping Growth/Scale as-is for prospects who convert up.
This is a positioning-risk call (Lane A: fewer but higher-value deals; Lane B: more top-of-funnel, harder to hold margin), not a pricing-accuracy one — flagging for founder sign-off rather than picking a default.

## 2. IT Services — ✅ Structural fix applied (per-user pricing)
| Tier | Scope | Indicative Price (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | Help-desk support, basic monitoring (small business, 10–50 users) | **$100–$175/user/mo** |
| Growth | Managed IT (monitoring, patching, backup, help-desk), defined SLA (mid-size, 50–250 users) | **$150–$250/user/mo** |
| Scale | Full managed IT + security posture + compliance support (compliance-heavy environments) | **$250–$400/user/mo** |
| **Entry offer** | Fixed-scope IT security/infrastructure audit | $1,500–$3,000 one-time |

This is a straight structural correction, not a repricing — the dollar totals were already roughly compatible with market (a 10-25 user client at the old flat tiers landed close to the new per-user math). The only change is presenting it the way every benchmarked competitor (Dataprise, VC3, Corsica, and 8+ MSP pricing guides) and every buyer already expects it. **Applied directly, no founder decision required** — this isn't a strategic choice, it's matching market convention.

## 7. Video Editing — ⚠ Founder decision needed (unchanged pending decision, widest gap in benchmark)
| Tier | Scope | Indicative Monthly Price (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | 4–6 edited videos/month | $1,000–$2,000 |
| Growth | 10–15 edited videos/month, platform-native variants | $2,500–$5,000 |
| Scale | High-volume ad-testing pipeline or ongoing content series | $5,500–$10,000+ |
| **Entry offer** | 5 ad variants or 1 demo video | $500–$1,200 one-time |

**The decision, made explicit:** Named competitors top out at $1,899–$3,000/mo (VideoScale's premium tier) — below Nivy Next's Growth tier *floor* ($2,500). Two lanes:
- **Lane A — Justify the premium:** Make the extra value explicit in the pitch — bundled design, higher production value, faster turnaround, dedicated editor vs. pooled queue. Keep pricing as-is.
- **Lane B — Reprice down:** Bring Growth tier floor closer to $1,899–$2,500 to sit at the top of the named-competitor range rather than above it, and rely on volume instead of per-deal margin.
Same structure as the Digital Marketing call above — flagging for founder sign-off, not deciding.

## Bundling Logic (Cross-Sell) — unchanged from v1
Entry offers are priced to convert into a Growth-tier retainer within 60–90 days. Multi-service bundles carry a **10–15% bundle discount** versus buying tiers separately — operationalizes "one partner, not five vendors" and counters the fragmented-delivery weakness identified in the SWOT.
