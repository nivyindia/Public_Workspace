# Industry Prioritization Scoring

**Module:** 02 — Market Intelligence | **Status:** v1 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-06

## Method
Each candidate industry is scored 1–5 on four weighted dimensions, using findings from the country snapshots and competitor benchmarking already completed:

| Dimension | Weight | What it Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Market Size & Growth | 30% | Addressable demand across the 5 target countries |
| Fit with Nivy Next's 7 Service Lines | 30% | How many service lines the industry plausibly buys (cross-sell potential) |
| Competitive Intensity | 20% | Lower score = more crowded/competed (inverted — 5 means *less* competition) |
| Margin/Budget Potential | 20% | Willingness and ability to pay for quality, not just cheapest vendor |

**Weighted Score = (Size×0.3) + (Fit×0.3) + (Competition×0.2) + (Margin×0.2)**

## Rationale by Top Priority

**1. SaaS / Tech Companies** — Largest and fastest-growing segment across US, UK, Canada; naturally buys across nearly all 7 service lines (web dev, IT, AI/automation, digital marketing, design, video for product marketing, and VA for ops); highest tolerance for premium, outcome-based pricing. Highest competitive intensity, but breadth-of-service positioning (Nivy Next's core differentiator per competitor SWOT) is a genuine wedge here since most competitors are single-discipline.

**2. E-commerce / DTC Brands** — Single largest client vertical by agency billings in the US (~20%); strong fit across web dev, digital marketing, design, video, and VA (customer service/ops). Price-sensitive at the low end but high-value at scale (Shopify Plus-tier brands).

**3. Professional Services Firms** — Present and buying in all 5 countries; strong fit for web dev, digital marketing, IT, and VA; historically underserved by AI/automation positioning — an opening given most competitors focus their AI messaging on tech-native buyers.

**4–5. Real Estate & Healthcare (non-clinical)** — Solid fit, particularly VA + digital marketing + web dev (real estate) and web dev + VA + compliance-aware marketing (healthcare admin). Healthcare scores lower on fit due to compliance overhead (HIPAA-adjacent concerns in the US) but scores well on margin and lower competitive intensity.

**6–8. Agencies (white-label), Hospitality, General Local SMEs** — Valid but lower-priority: white-label is a real channel opportunity (see UK snapshot and ColorWhistle competitive note) but is a *channel*, not a target *industry*, so treat as a parallel GTM motion rather than a primary ICP segment. Hospitality and generic local SMEs are viable secondary targets, particularly in Australia (Queensland tourism) and UAE, but score lowest on service-line fit and margin.

## Recommendation
**Prioritize SaaS, E-commerce, and Professional Services as the three lead industries for ICP development (Phase 3)**, with Real Estate and Healthcare as fast-follow secondary segments. Treat the white-label/agency-partner motion as a standing parallel channel initiative (already flagged in the UK country snapshot) rather than a fourth "industry."
